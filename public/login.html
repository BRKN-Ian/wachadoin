Onboarding a new employee

  1. Go to Team Members and add them with a name and email. Staff never get a dashboard password — they're identified only by their Agent Key.
  2. Click Agent Key next to their name. You'll see a download link, a Server URL, and a personal Agent Key.
  3. Send those three things to the employee (Slack, email, WhatsApp — whatever's convenient) and have them run the installer on their own computer.
  4. The one-time setup screen asks for the Server URL and Agent Key — once they paste those in and click Connect, it's done. The agent then runs quietly every time their computer starts; no login, ever.
  5. Before switching monitoring on for someone new, make sure they've had a monitoring notice / consent form covering your local privacy law — see the reminder at the bottom of this page.

Offboarding — when someone leaves

Go to Team Members and click Deactivate next to their name. This immediately:

  • blocks their access, even if the agent is mid-session;
  • stops the background agent from reporting anything further from their computer;
  • keeps their name and past time/activity records on file — nothing is deleted.

If you ever need their record gone entirely (e.g. a data-erasure request), use Delete permanently instead — but that's a one-way door, so Deactivate is the right first move in almost every case. Made a mistake? Reactivate restores their access.

How Monitoring Rules matching works

Wachadoin checks each employee's currently active window — the app name plus whatever's in its title bar (for a browser, that's usually the page or site name) — against the short words/phrases you've added under Work-related and Red flags. It's a simple "contains" check, not case-sensitive: adding slack matches "Slack | #general — Google Chrome" and would also match anything else with "slack" in it.

A sensible starting point for most businesses (or just click "+ Add suggested starter list" on that page):

  • Work-related: slack, microsoft teams, zoom, outlook, notion, asana, salesforce, github
  • Red flags: youtube, facebook, instagram, tiktok, netflix — plus job sites like indeed, linkedin jobs, glassdoor (useful for spotting morale issues early, not just distraction)

Quick answers

Someone shows "Offline" and never changes — check the agent is actually installed and running on their computer (Team Members → Agent Key has the download links again).

How long is data kept? — screenshots and raw activity delete themselves after 7 days automatically. Time entries and activity summaries are kept until you delete the person's account.

Do I need to tell staff about this? — yes — see the privacy-law reminder below.

What can a partner/director see that a manager can't? — a manager only sees the employees assigned to them; a partner/director sees every manager and every employee across the whole organization, and can add new managers.

Your legal obligations to staff

Monitoring employees' computers counts as processing their personal information under most data-privacy laws (for example South Africa's POPIA, or the GDPR in the EU/UK). You should give written notice, get acknowledgment, and add a monitoring clause to employment contracts before switching this on for someone new.

Before you start monitoring staff

Wachadoin captures screenshots, active-window titles, and activity levels from employees' computers. Under most data-privacy laws (e.g. South Africa's POPIA, the EU/UK GDPR, or similar laws elsewhere), this counts as processing employees' personal information, so a few things need to be in place before you switch it on:

1. Tell your employees

Give each employee written notice of what's monitored (screenshots, app/window titles, idle time), why, who can see it, and how long it's kept. Have them sign an acknowledgment.

2. Update employment contracts

Add a monitoring clause to new and existing employment contracts so consent is documented on file, not just a one-off form.

3. Limit and secure the data

Only collect what you need for legitimate work-performance purposes, restrict dashboard access to managers/partners who need it, and don't repurpose screenshots for anything unrelated to work performance.

This notice isn't legal advice — check which specific data-privacy law applies to your business and have a lawyer review the final wording for your contracts and consent forms.
